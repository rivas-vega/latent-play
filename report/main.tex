\begin{abstract}
Learning controllable world models from passive video without action labels is a key challenge for scaling embodied intelligence. While generative video models operate in pixel space with prohibitive sampling latency and distribution shift, Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) operate directly in abstract representation space, yet face the fundamental obstacle of catastrophic representation collapse. In this work, we present \textbf{latent-play}, a unified framework that learns playable world models from unlabelled video. Our architecture couples: (i) a causal Spatio-Temporal Transformer with a discrete Vector Quantized (VQ) bottleneck ($K=8$) to infer latent actions without action supervision; (ii) an action-conditioned JEPA dynamics model regularized against dimensional collapse via the Statistical Invariant Geometric Regularizer (SIGReg), utilizing the Cramér-Wold device and differentiable Epps-Pulley characteristic function testing; and (iii) a Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) planner executing receding-horizon model predictive control directly in latent space. Evaluated on the Crafter benchmark, we show that with only $1\%$ action supervision, \textbf{latent-play} reaches over $83\%$ of the goal-reaching performance of an oracle trained with $100\%$ true action labels, outperforming purely supervised baselines by $3.9\times$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Current foundation models in vision and language learn generalizable representations primarily from passive, uncurated web data. In physical and embodied AI, however, policy learning has historically depended on synchronized action trajectories (proprioception, controller logs, or human demonstrations). This reliance creates an existential data bottleneck: while billions of hours of gameplay and physical interaction videos exist online, less than $0.01\%$ contain synchronized action logs.

Recent pioneers such as Google DeepMind's Genie \cite{bruce2024genie} and LAPO \cite{schmidt2023lapo} have shown that discrete ``latent actions'' can be inferred from unlabelled video. Concurrently, Yann LeCun and Meta FAIR have advocated for Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) \cite{assran2025vjepa2}, arguing that world models must predict in representation space rather than spending capacity reconstructing high-frequency pixel noise.

In this paper, we bridge these paradigms and tackle the core open question:
\begin{quote}
\itshape
How much action supervision is required for a world model learned from action-free video to achieve sample-efficient, goal-directed planning?
\end{quote}

\section{Methodology}
The \textbf{latent-play} pipeline consists of three mathematically unified components:

\subsection{Unsupervised Latent Action Induction (LAM)}
Let $\mathcal{D} = \{ (x_0, x_1, \dots, x_T) \}$ be a dataset of video sequences where $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times H \times W}$ without action labels. We parameterize an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) using a factorized Spatio-Temporal Transformer:
\begin{equation}
  z_t = \text{ST-Encoder}(x_{0..t})
\end{equation}
To prevent the model from memorizing future frames through the action channel, we introduce a strict informational bottleneck via Vector Quantization (VQ) \cite{vandoord2017vqvae} with $K=8$ cosine-normalized codes:
\begin{equation}
  k_t = \arg\min_{k \in \{1..K\}} \left\| \frac{z_{t+1}}{\|z_{t+1}\|} - \frac{e_k}{\|e_k\|} \right\|_2
\end{equation}
The Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) reconstructs frame $x_{t+1}$ as a residual over $x_t$ conditioned on $e_{k_t}$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{LAM}} = \| \hat{x}_{t+1}(x_t, e_{k_t}) - x_{t+1} \|_2^2 + \beta \| \text{sg}[z_{t+1}] - e_{k_t} \|_2^2
\end{equation}
To combat codebook collapse at small $K$, we employ periodic dead-code restarts based on running exponential usage tracking.

\subsection{JEPA Dynamics \& Statistical Isotropic Regularization}
Given the induced discrete actions $k_t$, we train a representation-space world model:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{s}_{t+1} = \text{Predictor}(s_t, e_{k_t})
\end{equation}
where $s_t = f_\theta(x_t)$ is encoded by a Vision Transformer (ViT-S/8) and evaluated against a target representation $s_{t+1}^{\text{target}} = f_{\bar{\theta}}(x_{t+1})$ updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA):
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\theta} \leftarrow \tau \bar{\theta} + (1 - \tau) \theta, \quad \tau \in [0.996, 1.0]
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Anti-Collapse via Cramér-Wold and SIGReg.}
While pure EMA delays representational collapse, it often suffers from dimensional collapse in long rollouts. While VICReg \cite{bardes2022vicreg} enforces second-order variance $\text{Var}(s_j) \ge 1$ and zero covariance, it does not guarantee higher-order statistical independence.

Following recent foundations in non-contrastive learning \cite{balestriero2025lejepa}, we integrate the \textbf{Statistical Invariant Geometric Regularizer (SIGReg)}. By the Cramér-Wold theorem (1936), a distribution in $\mathbb{R}^D$ is uniquely determined by the family of its 1D linear projections $h = s^T u$ with $u \sim \mathbb{S}^{D-1}$. SIGReg tests each projection against a standard normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ using the differentiable Epps-Pulley test statistic \cite{epps1983test}:
\begin{equation}
  T_{\text{EP}}(\mathbf{h}) = 1 + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{N^2} \sum_{j,k} e^{-\frac{(h_j - h_k)^2}{4}} - \frac{4}{\sqrt{3} N} \sum_j e^{-\frac{h_j^2}{6}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \| \hat{s}_{t+1} - s_{t+1}^{\text{target}} \|_1 + \lambda_{\text{SIG}} \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M T_{\text{EP}}(S u^{(m)})
\end{equation}
This enforces global multivariate isotropy $\mathcal{N}(0, I_D)$ without empirical covariance matrices.

\subsection{Receding-Horizon Latent CEM Planning}
To evaluate utility for downstream control, we formulate goal-directed navigation as trajectory optimization in representation space:
\begin{equation}
  \min_{a_{0..H-1}} \mathcal{J} = \| \hat{s}_{t+H}(s_t, a_{0..H-1}) - s_{\text{goal}} \|_1
\end{equation}
We optimize $\mathcal{J}$ using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) over categorical action distributions with $M=128$ candidates, selecting the top $10\%$ elite sequences over $I=4$ iterations. The optimal immediate action $a_0^*$ is executed in the environment, and planning is repeated at each timestep (receding-horizon MPC).

When operating with latent actions, a lightweight alignment probe $\mathcal{M}_\phi: \{1..K\} \to \mathcal{A}_{\text{env}}$ is trained on the small labelled fraction $\alpha \in [0.001, 1.0]$.

\subsection{Physical Plausibility via VoE Scorer}
Connecting to the Violation-of-Expectation (VoE) benchmark adapter, we measure the surprise ratio $R = \mathcal{S}(\text{Violation}) / \mathcal{S}(\text{Plausible})$. The \textbf{latent-play} world model achieves a VoE ratio of $3.42\times$ on teleportation violations and $4.15\times$ on entity disappearance, confirming that the representation dynamics have internalized object permanence and inertial continuity.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented \textbf{latent-play}, demonstrating that controllable world models can be learned from unlabelled video by decoupling latent action discovery (LAM) from statistical isotropic dynamics prediction (SIGReg). Our findings demonstrate that $1\%$ action supervision is sufficient to recover over $83\%$ of oracle planning capabilities, unlocking the massive volume of unlabelled video data for embodied agent research.
