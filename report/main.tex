\begin{abstract}
Training controllable world models typically requires synchronized action logs $(x_t, a_t, x_{t+1})$, yet the vast majority of real-world and gameplay video consists of unlabelled pixel streams $(x_0, x_1, \dots, x_T)$. While Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid computationally intensive pixel-level reconstruction by predicting future states directly in representation space, they face two core challenges: (1) representation collapse when trained from scratch, and (2) reliance on ground-truth action labels that suffer from \emph{visual action aliasing} (e.g., pressing movement keys against obstacles or invoking crafting actions without prerequisites produces zero visual displacement). In this work, we present \textbf{latent-play}, a self-contained framework implemented and trained from scratch in PyTorch that learns playable world models from unlabelled video across four distinct stages: (i) an unsupervised \textbf{Latent Action Model (LAM)} that discovers $K=8$ discrete action codes using a Cosine Vector Quantizer regularized against codebook collapse; (ii) an action-conditioned \textbf{ViT-S/8 JEPA World Model} trained with both an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) target encoder to resolve the moving-target instability and \textbf{Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg)} to eliminate dimensional collapse; (iii) a few-shot \textbf{ActionAlignment} probe mapping discovered codes and scene context to game buttons with log-prior correction; and (iv) a test-time \textbf{Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)} planner operating in imagination entirely within the frozen latent space. Evaluated on the procedurally generated survival benchmark \textbf{Crafter} ($64\times 64$ RGB, 17 actions, $300\text{K}$ frames) and a 2D synthetic \textbf{ToyGridEnv}, our results show that: (1) discovered latent actions achieve $+10.05\text{ dB}$ controllability and $>95\%$ locomotion purity; (2) conditioning JEPA dynamics on discovered codes yields lower multi-step prediction error ($0.1523$ vs.\ $0.2003$ at $H=8$ on Crafter) by grouping transitions by visual consequence rather than aliased button presses; (3) action alignment saturates with just $1\%$ supervision ($\sim 2{,}600$ frames), reaching the same closed-loop planning success as a $100\%$-supervised Action Oracle while roughly doubling random-walk success; and (4) the learned dynamics detect physical anomalies zero-shot with Violation-of-Expectation (VoE) surprise ratios of \textbf{15.25$\times$ on teleportation} and \textbf{52.91$\times$ on object disappearance}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Problem Formulation}
Standard model-based reinforcement learning and action-conditioned generative world models \cite{hafner2023dreamerv3,assran2025vjepa2} assume that every visual transition $(x_t, x_{t+1})$ is paired with an explicit controller button label $a_t \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{env}}$. In practice, massive internet-scale video datasets and passive observations are completely unlabelled ($a_t = \varnothing$).

To turn unlabelled video into an actionable, plannable world model, an agent must decouple two distinct problems:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Unsupervised Action Discovery:} Inferring a compact set of discrete latent codes $k_t \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$ that account for the controllable visual motion between consecutive frames $(x_t, x_{t+1})$, without access to game rules, inventories, or keypress logs \cite{bruce2024genie,schmidt2023lapo,ye2025lapa}.
  \item \textbf{Non-Collapsing Predictive Dynamics:} Learning an encoder $z_t = f_\theta(x_t)$ and a transition predictor $\hat{z}_{t+1} = g_\psi(z_t, k_t)$ in an abstract representation space without pixel generation, while preventing both trivial point collapse ($z \equiv \mathbf{c}$) and low-rank dimensional collapse \cite{assran2023ijepa,balestriero2025lejepa,bardes2022vicreg}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{All Models Trained From Scratch (\emph{Tabula Rasa}).}
In contrast to prior works that fine-tune frozen web-scale vision models (e.g., DINOv2 or ImageNet backbones), \textbf{all neural components in \texttt{latent-play} are implemented and trained from random initialization in PyTorch}. Our architecture comprises a custom Spatio-Temporal Latent Action Model, a Vision Transformer (\texttt{ViT-S/8}) dynamics network, and statistical regularizers analyzed in the author's prior mathematical work on self-supervised representation collapse.

\section{Benchmark Environments and Protocol}
We validate our framework across two distinct domains stored as memory-mapped \texttt{uint8} shards (\texttt{np.memmap}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Crafter ($300{,}000$ steps, $1{,}636$ episodes):} The open-ended survival benchmark \cite{hafner2021crafter} ($64 \times 64$ RGB, 17 discrete actions: 4 cardinal movements, \texttt{noop}, \texttt{do}, \texttt{sleep}, and 10 placement/crafting actions). Crafter features procedural terrain generation, day-night lighting cycles, health and hunger dynamics, and complex crafting trees. Transitions are collected using an exploratory policy that repeats directional movements for $2\text{--}6$ steps ($80\%$ probability) interleaved with interaction actions ($20\%$).
  \item \textbf{ToyGridEnv ($100{,}000$ steps, $1{,}540$ episodes):} A synthetic $64 \times 64$ RGB arena with 5 discrete actions (\texttt{noop, up, down, left, right}). An agent-controlled cyan square interacts against an autonomously moving orange distractor with randomized per-episode background noise.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Strict Zero-Leakage Guarantee.}
To guarantee that unsupervised stages receive zero action supervision, every transition is assigned a persistent pseudo-random uniform draw $u_i \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)$ upon creation. During dataset loading with supervision fraction $\alpha$, transitions with $u_i \ge \alpha$ return $\texttt{action} = -1$. Stages 1 and 2 operate under $\alpha = 0.0$ ($100\%$ unlabelled).

\section{Methodology: The Four Stages}
The complete framework is summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:arch} and specified in Table~\ref{tab:master}. Figure~\ref{fig:walkthrough} illustrates the conceptual visual progression across all four stages.

\subsection{Stage 1: Latent Action Discovery (\texttt{LAM})}
\paragraph{Inverse Dynamics and Motion Attention.}
Given consecutive frames $(x_t, x_{t+1}) \in [0, 1]^{2 \times 3 \times 64 \times 64}$, a convolutional patch embedding layer ($8 \times 8$ patches, stride $8$) produces $N_p = 64$ spatial tokens of dimension $D$ ($D=128$ on ToyGrid, $D=256$ on Crafter). Tokens pass through 4 factorized Spatio-Temporal residual blocks (\texttt{STBlock}) with causal temporal attention. To extract what physically moved while ignoring static background scenery, we compute the temporal feature delta $\Delta h_t = h_{t+1} - h_t$ and pool across spatial patches via learned motion attention:
\begin{equation}
  u_t = \text{LN}\!\left(\sum_{p=1}^{64} \text{softmax}_p(w_{\text{attn}}^\top \Delta h_{t,p}) \, \Delta h_{t,p}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^D.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Anti-Collapse Cosine Vector Quantizer.}
A linear projection maps $u_t$ to $z_e \in \mathbb{R}^{d_c}$ ($d_c = 32$). Standard VQ-VAEs \cite{vandoord2017vqvae} commonly suffer from \emph{codebook collapse}, where the model clusters all transitions into only 1 or 2 codes. We prevent collapse through three complementary mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Batch-Mean Centering:} We center incoming vectors $\tilde{z}_e = z_e - \bar{z}_e$ to eliminate global bias.
  \item \textbf{Cosine Hypersphere Quantization:} Nearest-neighbor lookup over $K=8$ codebook vectors $e_k \in \mathbb{S}^{d_c-1}$:
  \begin{equation}
    k_t = \arg\max_{k \in \{0..7\}} \frac{\tilde{z}_e^\top e_k}{\|\tilde{z}_e\|_2 \, \|e_k\|_2}.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Differentiable Batch-Entropy Regularization:} Over soft assignment probabilities $p_k = \text{softmax}(\cos(\tilde{z}_e, e_k)/\tau_{\text{vq}})$, we maximize batch marginal entropy $H(\bar{p})$ while minimizing conditional entropy $H(p \mid \tilde{z}_e)$:
  \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{ent}} = H(p \mid \tilde{z}_e) - H(\bar{p}), \quad \bar{p} = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B p^{(b)}.
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
This forces the model to distribute transitions uniformly across all $8/8$ codes (perplexity $7.12 / 8.0$ on Crafter).

\paragraph{Why $K=8$ Latent Codes for Crafter's 17 Actions?}
In unlabelled video, the model can only perceive \emph{visual changes}. In Crafter, 12 of the 17 discrete actions are crafting or placement commands that produce zero visual difference when preconditions are missing (e.g., attempting to craft without wood), while walking into trees or walls produces zero camera translation. Thus, the 17 game buttons collapse into a much smaller set of distinct visual kinematic primitives (directional camera moves, screen darkening on sleep, and stationary/interaction clusters). Choosing $K=8$ captures all visual modes without codebook dilution, keeps CEM planning search tractable ($8^6 \approx 2.6\times 10^5$ candidate paths vs. $17^6 \approx 2.4\times 10^7$), and lets the Stage 3 probe resolve the full 17 actions by conditioning on visual scene context $\bar{z}_t$.

\paragraph{Forward Dynamics Pixel Decoder.}
A 4-layer spatial Transformer decoder conditions on $(x_t, e_{k_t})$ to reconstruct $\hat{x}_{t+1}$. We apply motion-weighted MSE to prioritize moving entities over static terrain:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{mot}} = \mathbb{E}\Big[\big(1 + 10\,\|x_{t+1} - x_t\|_1\big)\,\|\hat{x}_{t+1} - x_{t+1}\|_2^2\Big].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 2: Isotropic JEPA World Model (\texttt{JEPADynamics})}
Once Stage 1 finishes, \texttt{LAM} is frozen and acts as an unsupervised pseudo-labeler.

\paragraph{Online Encoder, Predictor, and Target Network.}
The online visual encoder $f_\theta$ (\texttt{ViT-S/8}, $N_p=64$ patches, $D=192$ on ToyGrid, $D=384$ on Crafter) maps $x_t \to z_t \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times D}$. The causal predictor $g_\psi$ embeds $k_t$ via lookup table $E_a \in \mathbb{R}^{8 \times D}$, adds it to the patch tokens, and predicts future latents $\hat{z}_{t+1}$. Ground-truth prediction targets $z_{t+1}^{\text{tgt}} = \text{sg}[f_{\bar{\theta}}(x_{t+1})]$ are provided by a target encoder $f_{\bar{\theta}}$ updated via cosine-scheduled Exponential Moving Average ($\tau \in [0.996, 1.0]$).

\paragraph{Why Both EMA and SIGReg? (Two Distinct Collapse Modes).}
In static image self-supervised learning \cite{balestriero2025lejepa,bardes2022vicreg}, an explicit covariance penalty alone can prevent collapse without an EMA target. However, in an \textbf{autoregressive, action-conditioned World Model}, two distinct failure modes arise:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The Moving Target Problem (solved by EMA):} If the predictor $g_\psi$ predicts features directly from an online encoder $f_\theta$ that updates at every gradient step, the target distribution drifts rapidly. This creates severe training instability and rollout divergence across multi-step horizons. The EMA target encoder provides a slowly-evolving, stable target anchor \cite{grill2020byol}.
  \item \textbf{Dimensional Collapse (solved by SIGReg):} While an EMA target prevents \emph{point collapse} ($z \equiv \mathbf{c}$), it is prone to \emph{dimensional collapse}, where representations collapse onto a low-dimensional subspace (effective rank drops from 384 down to $\sim 4$). When dimensions collapse, different actions predict virtually indistinguishable future states.
\end{enumerate}
To guarantee full-rank representations without $O(D^3)$ covariance matrix inversions, we apply \textbf{Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg)} \cite{balestriero2025lejepa}. By the Cram\'er-Wold theorem \cite{cramer1936theorems}, a $D$-dimensional distribution matches the standard normal $\mathcal{N}(0, I_D)$ if and only if all 1D projections $h^{(m)} = Z u^{(m)}$ along unit directions $u^{(m)} \sim \mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ follow $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$. For $M=64$ random projections, we evaluate the differentiable Epps-Pulley empirical characteristic function test \cite{epps1983test}:
\begin{equation}
  T_{\text{EP}}(\mathbf{h}) = 1 + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{N^2} \sum_{j,k=1}^N e^{-\frac{(h_j - h_k)^2}{4}} - \frac{4}{\sqrt{3} N} \sum_{j=1}^N e^{-\frac{h_j^2}{6}},
\end{equation}
and optimize the composite objective:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{JEPA}} &= \|\hat{z} - \text{sg}[z^{\text{tgt}}]\|_1 \\
  &\quad + \frac{\lambda_{\text{SIG}}}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M T_{\text{EP}}(Z u^{(m)}) + \lambda_{\text{var}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}(Z).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This maintains an effective rank of $39.1 / 384$ throughout $30{,}000$ training steps on Crafter.

\subsection{Stage 3: Few-Shot Action Alignment (\texttt{ActionAlignment})}
With LAM and JEPA frozen, we train a lightweight 2-layer MLP probe ($1.6\text{K}$ parameters) on a tiny fraction $\alpha \in \{0.1\%, 1\%, 5\%, 10\%, 100\%\}$ of labelled interaction frames. The probe predicts real environment buttons $P_\phi(a_{\text{env}} \mid k_t, \bar{z}_t)$ conditioned on both the discrete code $k_t$ and the mean-pooled scene context $\bar{z}_t = \frac{1}{64}\sum_p z_{t,p}$. Context conditioning is essential in Crafter because pressing "move up" produces upward motion on open grass but zero motion against a stone wall. To correct for class imbalance (e.g., frequent stationary actions), we apply log-prior adjustment at test time:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{a}_t = \arg\max_{a \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{env}}} \big(\log P_\phi(a \mid k_t, \bar{z}_t) - \gamma \log \hat{P}(a)\big), \quad \gamma = 0.5.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 4: Latent Closed-Loop Planning (\texttt{CEMPlanner})}
At test time, given the live observation $x_t$ and goal image $x_{\text{goal}}$, \texttt{CEMPlanner} operates entirely in imagination inside the frozen JEPA latent space without pixel generation. Over $I=4$ iterations, it samples $M=128$ candidate 6-step latent sequences, evaluates their trajectory distance to $z_{\text{goal}} = f_\theta(x_{\text{goal}})$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{J} = 0.5\,\|\hat{z}_{t+H} - z_{\text{goal}}\|_1 + 0.5\min_{1 \le h \le H}\|\hat{z}_{t+h} - z_{\text{goal}}\|_1,
\end{equation}
selects elite sequences, executes the probe-aligned action for the first code $k_0^*$, and replans in a receding-horizon fashion.

\paragraph{Visual Action Aliasing and Latent Rollout Predictability.}
Conditioning \texttt{JEPADynamics} on discovered LAM codes yields lower multi-step latent prediction error than conditioning on ground-truth actions ($0.1523$ vs.\ $0.2003$ at $H=8$ on Crafter). This occurs because ground-truth supervision suffers from \emph{visual action aliasing}: failed crafting commands and blocked movements map distinct action IDs to identical static transitions, whereas LAM groups transitions by realized visual consequence.
\emph{Methodological Caveat:} Because each model trains its own encoder pair $(f_\theta, f_{\bar{\theta}})$, latent $L_1$ errors are measured in each model's respective representation space and should always be interpreted alongside downstream closed-loop control.

\subsection{Zero-Shot Violation-of-Expectation (VoE) Physics}
As reported in Table~\ref{tab:pretrain} and Figure~\ref{fig:purity}b, \texttt{JEPADynamics} internalizes physical plausibility zero-shot. When non-inertial spatial teleportation is introduced, latent prediction surprise jumps from $0.0209$ to $0.2862$ ($\mathbf{15.25\times}$). When an entity abruptly vanishes, violating object permanence, surprise jumps to $0.9016$ ($\mathbf{52.91\times}$). Action contradiction increases surprise by $1.83\times$, confirming that the model's representations encode spatial continuity and physical conservation laws.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Latent Action Models (LAMs).}
Unsupervised action inference from passive video has gained prominence through Genie \cite{bruce2024genie}, LAPO \cite{schmidt2023lapo}, and LAPA \cite{ye2025lapa}. These works rely on autoregressive token generation or pixel-level diffusion, which can be computationally prohibitive. In contrast, \texttt{latent-play} integrates discrete latent action discovery directly with non-generative representation-space dynamics.

\paragraph{Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs).}
Introduced in I-JEPA \cite{assran2023ijepa} and extended to video in V-JEPA \cite{assran2025vjepa2}, JEPAs predict representations of masked spatio-temporal blocks without pixel reconstruction. LeJEPA \cite{balestriero2025lejepa} and VICReg \cite{bardes2022vicreg} established non-contrastive covariance regularizers to prevent collapse in static vision. We extend this foundation to action-conditioned autoregressive world models, demonstrating that combining an EMA target network (solving moving-target drift) with SIGReg (solving dimensional collapse) enables stable multi-step planning from scratch.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented \textbf{latent-play}, demonstrating that controllable, non-collapsing world models can be learned tabula rasa from unlabelled video. By uniting an entropy-regularized Cosine VQ latent action model with an isotropic JEPA dynamics predictor and a few-shot alignment probe, \texttt{latent-play} matches the closed-loop navigation performance of fully supervised world models using only $1\%$ of action labels, while discovering emergent physical continuity zero-shot.
