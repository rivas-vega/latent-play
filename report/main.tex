\begin{abstract}
Training controllable world models typically requires synchronized action logs $(x_t, a_t, x_{t+1})$, yet $99.99\%$ of real-world and gameplay video consists of unlabelled pixel streams $(x_0, x_1, \dots, x_T)$. While Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) avoid slow pixel-level generation by predicting future states in representation space, they face two fundamental challenges: (1) catastrophic representation collapse when trained from scratch, and (2) reliance on ground-truth action labels that suffer from \emph{visual action aliasing} (e.g., pressing movement keys against obstacles or invoking context-dependent actions without prerequisites produces zero visual displacement). In this work, we present \textbf{latent-play}, a fully self-contained framework implemented and trained from scratch in PyTorch that learns playable world models from unlabelled video in three decoupled stages: (i) an unsupervised \textbf{Latent Action Model (LAM)} with a causal Spatio-Temporal Transformer and an entropy-regularized Vector Quantized (VQ, $K=8$) bottleneck; (ii) an action-conditioned \textbf{ViT-S/8 JEPA World Model} regularized against dimensional collapse via the \textbf{Statistical Invariant Geometric Regularizer (SIGReg)} using the Cram\'er-Wold theorem and differentiable Epps-Pulley normality testing; and (iii) a few-shot \textbf{ActionAlignment} probe with log-prior class-imbalance correction coupled with a receding-horizon \textbf{Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)} planner. Across \textbf{ToyGridEnv} ($100\text{K}$ frames) and \textbf{Crafter} ($300\text{K}$ frames, $30\text{K}$ steps on an NVIDIA A100), we show that: (1) discovered latent actions achieve $+10.05\text{ dB}$ controllability ($\Delta\text{PSNR}_{\text{ctrl}}$) and $95.6\%$ locomotion purity; (2) conditioning JEPA dynamics on discovered LAM codes reduces 8-step rollout error by \textbf{24.0\% on Crafter} ($0.1523$ vs.\ $0.2003$) and 4-step error by \textbf{38.3\% on ToyGridEnv} compared to a $100\%$-supervised Action Oracle; (3) with only $0.1\%\text{--}10\%$ action labels, \textbf{latent-play} matches or exceeds the $100\%$ Action Oracle in closed-loop CEM navigation ($28.0\%$ mean / $36.0\%$ peak on ToyGrid; $15.6\%$ mean / $26.7\%$ peak on Crafter vs.\ $10.0\%$ Oracle); and (4) the learned dynamics detect physical violations zero-shot with Violation-of-Expectation (VoE) surprise ratios of \textbf{15.25$\times$ on teleportation} and \textbf{52.91$\times$ on object disappearance}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Problem Formulation}
Standard model-based reinforcement learning assumes access to action-annotated transitions $\mathcal{D}_{\text{sup}} = \{(x_t, a_t, x_{t+1})\}$. In contrast, passive video datasets provide only raw RGB observations $\mathcal{D}_{\text{vid}} = \{(x_0, x_1, \dots, x_T)\}$ with $x_t \in [0, 1]^{3 \times H \times W}$ and no action labels ($a_t = \varnothing$).

To turn passive video into a \emph{playable, plannable world model}, two distinct problems must be solved:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Unsupervised Action Discovery:} Inferring a compact discrete variable $k_t \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$ between $x_t$ and $x_{t+1}$ that captures the controllable agent-induced transition while ignoring background noise and autonomous distractors \cite{bruce2024genie,schmidt2023lapo}.
  \item \textbf{Non-Collapsing Representation Dynamics:} Learning an encoder $z_t = f_\theta(x_t)$ and transition predictor $\hat{z}_{t+1} = g_\psi(z_{0..t}, k_{0..t})$ directly in latent space \cite{assran2025vjepa2,balestriero2025lejepa} without trivial constant collapse ($f_\theta(x) \equiv \mathbf{c}$) or dimensional subspace collapse.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Origin of Our Architecture (Trained From Scratch).}
Unlike works that fine-tune frozen web-scale encoders, \textbf{all models in \texttt{latent-play} are implemented and trained from scratch (random initialization) in PyTorch} on our collected $64 \times 64$ video datasets. Specifically, we design a custom ViT-S/8 Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (\texttt{JEPADynamics}) inspired by V-JEPA 2-AC \cite{assran2025vjepa2} and couple it with \textbf{SIGReg} \cite{balestriero2025lejepa,epps1983test}—a statistical isotropy regularizer analyzed in the author's Bachelor's Thesis (\texttt{TFG\_Beamer})—to guarantee full-rank latent representations without contrastive negative sampling.

\section{Data Generation and Zero-Leakage Protocol}
We collect two datasets stored as memory-mapped \texttt{uint8} shards (\texttt{np.memmap}) with episode-level $95\%/5\%$ train/validation splits:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ToyGridEnv ($100{,}000$ steps, $1{,}540$ episodes):} A $64 \times 64$ RGB arena with 5 discrete actions (\texttt{noop, up, down, left, right}). The player controls a $5\times 5$ cyan square, while a $5\times 5$ orange distractor moves autonomously at random and background noise varies per episode.
  \item \textbf{Crafter ($300{,}000$ steps, $1{,}636$ episodes):} The open-ended survival benchmark \cite{hafner2021crafter} ($64 \times 64$ RGB, 17 actions: 4 cardinal movements, \texttt{noop}, \texttt{do}, \texttt{sleep}, and 10 placement/crafting actions). To ensure rich spatial coverage across water, forests, caves, and quarries, data is collected via an exploratory segment policy that repeats directional movements for $2\text{--}6$ steps ($80\%$ probability) interleaved with interaction actions ($20\%$).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Zero-Leakage Guarantee:} Each transition is assigned a fixed uniform draw $u_i \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)$ at collection time. When training with supervision fraction $\alpha$, transitions with $u_i \ge \alpha$ return \texttt{action = -1}. Stages 1 and 2 run with $\alpha = 0.0$.

\section{Didactic Methodology: Step-by-Step}
Table~\ref{tab:master} summarizes the inputs, outputs, learnable parameters, and objectives of each stage. Below we detail the mathematical mechanics of each module.

\subsection{Stage 1: Unsupervised Latent Action Model (LAM)}
\paragraph{Inputs and Tokenization.}
Given a clip of $T=3$ consecutive frames $(x_{t-1}, x_t, x_{t+1}) \in [0, 1]^{B \times 3 \times 3 \times 64 \times 64}$, a convolutional \texttt{PatchEmbed} layer ($8 \times 8$ stride $8$) maps each frame into $N_p = 64$ spatial patch tokens of dimension $D$ ($D=128$ on ToyGrid, $D=256$ on Crafter), adding fixed 2D sine-cosine spatial positional encodings and learned temporal embeddings.

\paragraph{Causal Inverse Dynamics \& Motion Pooling.}
The tokens pass through 4 factorized Spatio-Temporal (\texttt{STBlock}) layers: within each layer, tokens first attend bidirectionally across all $64$ patches of the same frame $t$, and then attend causally across time ($\le t$) at the same spatial patch location. To isolate what changed between frame $t$ and $t+1$ while discarding static background features, we take the temporal feature difference $\Delta h_t = h_{t+1} - h_t \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 64 \times D}$ and compute a learned spatial attention pooling:
\begin{equation}
  u_t = \text{LayerNorm}\left(\sum_{p=1}^{64} \text{softmax}_p(w_{\text{attn}}^\top \Delta h_{t,p}) \, \Delta h_{t,p}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times D}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Anti-Collapse Cosine Vector Quantizer (VQ).}
A linear projection maps $u_t$ to $z_e \in \mathbb{R}^{d_c}$ ($d_c = 32$). Because consecutive frames in video are highly correlated, standard VQ-VAEs \cite{vandoord2017vqvae} frequently collapse to a single code ($1/K$ usage). We prevent codebook collapse via three mechanisms: (1) subtracting the batch mean $\tilde{z}_e = z_e - \bar{z}_e$ before quantization; (2) cosine-normalized nearest-neighbor lookup over $K=8$ codes $k_t = \arg\min_k \|\tilde{z}_e/\|\tilde{z}_e\|_2 - e_k/\|e_k\|_2\|_2$; and (3) a differentiable soft-assignment entropy regularizer over cosine similarities $p_k = \text{softmax}(\text{sim}(\tilde{z}_e, e_k) / \tau_{\text{vq}})$ that maximizes marginal batch entropy $H(\bar{p})$ (encouraging uniform $8/8$ code usage) while minimizing conditional entropy $H(p \mid \tilde{z}_e)$ (encouraging sharp, confident assignments):
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{ent}} = H(p \mid \tilde{z}_e) - H(\bar{p}), \quad \bar{p} = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B p^{(b)}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Motion-Weighted Forward Decoder.}
A 4-layer spatial Transformer decoder takes the current frame tokens $x_t$ conditioned on the straight-through quantized action vector $z_q = \tilde{z}_e + \text{sg}[e_{k_t} - \tilde{z}_e]$ and outputs a pixel residual $\hat{x}_{t+1} = \text{clamp}(x_t + \Delta\hat{x}_t, 0, 1)$. To force the 3-bit code $k_t$ to prioritize moving entities (the player) over static grass/background, we weight the pixel MSE by local frame difference:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{motion-MSE}} = \frac{1}{B H W}\sum \big(1 + 10\,\|x_{t+1} - x_t\|_1\big)\,\|\hat{x}_{t+1} - x_{t+1}\|_2^2.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 2: Action-Conditioned JEPA \& SIGReg}
\paragraph{Online Encoder, EMA Target, and Residual Predictor.}
Once Stage 1 finishes, \texttt{LAM} is frozen. Given 8-frame video clips $x_{0..7}$, the frozen \texttt{LAM} labels each transition with its discrete latent action $k_t \in \{0..7\}$. Our online encoder $f_\theta$ (\texttt{ViT-S/8}, $N_p=64$ patches, $D=192$ on ToyGrid, $D=384$ on Crafter) encodes frames into patch representations $z_t = f_\theta(x_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times D}$. A target encoder $f_{\bar{\theta}}$ with identical architecture produces ground-truth future targets $z_{t+1}^{\text{tgt}} = \text{sg}[f_{\bar{\theta}}(x_{t+1})]$, where $\bar{\theta}$ is updated via cosine-scheduled Exponential Moving Average ($\tau \in [0.996, 1.0]$).

The predictor $g_\psi$ embeds the discrete action $k_t$ via a lookup table $E_a(k_t) \in \mathbb{R}^D$, adds it to the spatial patch tokens of $z_t$, processes the sequence through causal \texttt{STBlock} layers, and predicts the residual transition:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{z}_{t+1} = z_t + \Delta z_{\psi}(z_{0..t}, k_{0..t}).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Preventing Collapse with SIGReg (Cram\'er-Wold + Epps-Pulley).}
To prevent $f_\theta(x_t)$ from collapsing to a constant or low-rank subspace without OOM-inducing $D \times D$ covariance matrices, we regularize the online tokens $z_t$ (subsampled to $N=512$ tokens per step) using \textbf{SIGReg} \cite{balestriero2025lejepa}. By the Cram\'er-Wold theorem, a $D$-dimensional distribution matches the isotropic standard normal $\mathcal{N}(0, I_D)$ if and only if all 1D unit projections $h^{(m)} = Z u^{(m)}$ ($u^{(m)} \sim \mathbb{S}^{D-1}$) follow $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$. For $M=64$ random projections, we compute the differentiable Epps-Pulley characteristic function statistic \cite{epps1983test}:
\begin{equation}
  T_{\text{EP}}(\mathbf{h}) = 1 + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{N^2} \sum_{j,k=1}^N e^{-\frac{(h_j - h_k)^2}{4}} - \frac{4}{\sqrt{3} N} \sum_{j=1}^N e^{-\frac{h_j^2}{6}},
\end{equation}
and minimize the combined JEPA objective:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{JEPA}} = \| \hat{z}_{1..T} - \text{sg}[z_{1..T}^{\text{tgt}}] \|_1 + \frac{\lambda_{\text{SIG}}}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M T_{\text{EP}}(Z u^{(m)}) + \lambda_{\text{var}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}(Z).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 3 \& 4: Few-Shot Alignment \& Latent CEM}
Because the world model plans over abstract latent codes $k_t \in \{0..7\}$, we train a lightweight $1.6\text{K}$-parameter 2-layer MLP (\texttt{ActionAlignment}) on a small labelled subset $\alpha \in \{0.1\%, 1\%, 5\%, 10\%, 100\%\}$ to predict the real environment action $P_\phi(a_{\text{env}} \mid k_t, \bar{z}_t)$ from the one-hot code $k_t$ and mean-pooled state $\bar{z}_t$. To prevent frequent stationary actions (\texttt{noop}, \texttt{do}) in Crafter from dominating movement codes, we apply log-prior adjustment at inference:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{a}_t = \arg\max_{a \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{env}}} \left( \log P_\phi(a \mid k_t, \bar{z}_t) - \gamma \log \hat{P}(a) \right), \quad \gamma = 0.5.
\end{equation}
At test time, given current frame $x_t$ and goal frame $x_{\text{goal}}$, \texttt{CEMPlanner} encodes $z_t = f_\theta(x_t)$ and $z_{\text{goal}} = f_\theta(x_{\text{goal}})$, iteratively refines categorical logits over $H$ future steps by rolling out $M=128$ latent trajectories inside $g_\psi$, selects the elite sequences minimizing combined terminal and closest-approach $L_1$ distance to $z_{\text{goal}}$, executes the aligned action $\hat{a}_0$, and replans at $t+1$.

\section{Empirical Results and Analysis}

\subsection{Why Latent Actions Beat True Actions in Rollouts}
Table~\ref{tab:rollouts} and Figure~\ref{fig:anticollapse}a reveal a remarkable finding: on both ToyGridEnv and Crafter, conditioning \texttt{JEPADynamics} on unsupervised LAM codes achieves \textbf{significantly lower multi-step rollout error} than the supervised Action Oracle ($0.1523$ vs.\ $0.2003$ at $H=8$ on Crafter, $-24.0\%$; $0.1416$ vs.\ $0.2297$ at $H=4$ on ToyGrid, $-38.3\%$).

\textbf{Physical Explanation (Visual Action Aliasing):} In Crafter, $12$ of the $17$ actions are context-dependent crafting/placement commands that produce zero visual change when prerequisites are absent, and the $4$ movement actions produce zero displacement when blocked by water, trees, or walls. Consequently, the Action Oracle receives contradictory supervision where distinct action IDs map to identical static transitions. Conversely, \texttt{LAM} groups transitions by their \emph{realized visual effect}—as confirmed by our contingency matrix where codes $5, 6, 7$ achieve $80.3\%, 95.6\%, 94.7\%$ pure locomotion—yielding a de-aliased, deterministic transition model.

\subsection{Sample-Efficient CEM Planning \& VoE Physics}
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:results} and Figure~\ref{fig:scaling}, with just $0.1\%$ action labels ($92$ transitions), \textbf{latent-play} matches the $100\%$ Action Oracle on ToyGridEnv ($28.0\%$ mean, $36.0\%$ best seed). On Crafter, \textbf{latent-play} achieves $12.2\%\text{--}15.6\%$ mean success (peaking at $23.3\%$ with $1\%$ labels and $26.7\%$ with $10\%$ labels), surpassing the $10.0\%$ Action Oracle. Finally, on the zero-shot Violation-of-Expectation (\texttt{VoE}) benchmark (Figure~\ref{fig:purity}b), latent prediction surprise jumps from $0.0209$ on plausible clips to $0.2862$ on spatial teleportation ($\mathbf{15.25\times}$) and $0.9016$ on sudden object disappearance ($\mathbf{52.91\times}$), demonstrating emergent object permanence and spatiotemporal continuity.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented \textbf{latent-play}, proving that playable, non-collapsing world models can be trained from scratch on unlabelled video by uniting entropy-regularized motion VQ bottlenecks with SIGReg-regularized JEPA dynamics.
